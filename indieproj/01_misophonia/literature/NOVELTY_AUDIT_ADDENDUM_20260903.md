## 1. What has already been done (verified)

| Claim | Study | Verified against | What it actually found |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bodily-sensation / interoceptive sensitivity is elevated in misophonia | Misophonia community-sample profile analysis, N=628 (121 elevated-misophonia, 507 non-misophonia), *Behaviour Research and Therapy*, 2018 | [PubMed 29530266](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29530266/) | A profile using the ASI-3 **Physical Concerns** subscale found a misophonia-elevated profile (11% of variance) with significantly elevated physical/interoceptive-sensation concerns. | Anxiety sensitivity relates to misophonia and to aggression | *Evaluating anxiety sensitivity as a moderator of misophonia and dimensions of aggression*, 2021 | [ScienceDirect S2211364921000373](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2211364921000373) | Anxiety sensitivity examined as a moderator between misophonia and aggression dimensions — establishes A-adjacent measurement (ASI-3-type constructs) already in the literature, but as a moderator of a different outcome (aggression, not severity). |
| Anxiety sensitivity and distress intolerance relate to misophonia maintenance/treatment in youth | Cepeda et al., *Journal of Clinical Psychology*, 2026 | [Wiley 10.1002/jclp.70095](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jclp.70095) | Recent (2026) confirmation that anxiety-sensitivity-type constructs remain an active line in misophonia research, now extended to youth and to distress intolerance — the A side of this model is not stale. |
| **Closest existing precedent** — appraisal/emotional-process variables, alongside anxiety sensitivity and interoception, predicting misophonia impairment | Wang, Vitoratou et al., *Emotion Processes Predicting Outbursts and Functional Impact in Misophonia*, N=703 (315 misophonia), *Frontiers in Psychology*, 2022 | [PMC9292922](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9292922/) / [PubMed 35859827](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35859827/) / [KCL portal](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/emotion-processes-predicting-outbursts-and-functional-impact-in-m/) | Tested anxiety sensitivity, interoception, emotional threat appraisal, and externalising appraisal against two outcomes (outbursts, functional impact). **Key result, confirmed**: externalising appraisal predicted outbursts (partly via anxiety symptoms and disgust sensitivity); emotional threat appraisal predicted functional impact (partly via depression and negative beliefs about emotions). **Anxiety sensitivity and interoception were *not* significant independent predictors of either outcome.** |

## 2. What has *not* been done (the actual gap, stated carefully)

None of the above studies:
- entered a registration-type measure (MAIA-2/BPQ-style bodily-sensibility, not ASI-3-as-proxy) and an appraisal-type measure **simultaneously in one competing model** against misophonia *severity* specifically (as opposed to outbursts or aggression);
- tested whether the two are **empirically discriminable constructs** before treating them as separable predictors (the gap this project's own §3.4 discriminant-validity check is designed to close);
- tested an explicit **gating/moderation form** (registration converts to severity only when appraised as threatening) as a pre-specified functional form, distinct from a simple additive or amplification pattern.

The 2022 Wang/Vitoratou finding is the single most load-bearing piece of prior evidence for this project's framing: **it already shows anxiety sensitivity and interoception failing as independent predictors once appraisal-type variables are in the model** — which is consistent with (not proof of) this project's central claim that appraisal, not registration, carries the predictive weight. That paper used aggression/impairment outcomes and different instruments; this project tests the same logical structure against misophonia severity (AMISOS-R) using MAIA-2/BPQ (registration) and ASI-3 (appraisal), which has not been done.

## 3. Revised novelty statement (replaces the "first competitive test of an existing cognitive model" framing where a sharper claim is wanted)

> Prior work has independently implicated interoceptive/bodily sensitivity, anxiety sensitivity, and cognitive-emotional appraisal in misophonia, and one close precedent (Wang, Vitoratou et al., 2022) found that anxiety sensitivity and interoception drop out as predictors once appraisal-type variables are modeled — but no study has entered registration and appraisal as a single competing model against misophonia severity, established that the two are discriminable constructs, and tested whether appraisal *gates* (rather than merely adds to) the conversion of registered bodily sensation into clinical severity.

This is a stronger, more specific novelty claim than "first competitive test of a cognitive model" — it names exactly which combination of design features (severity as outcome + discriminability check + gating-form test) is new, and it is falsifiable by a citation in a way a vaguer claim is not.

**One live risk, stated honestly**: the Wang/Vitoratou null result for anxiety sensitivity and interoception could mean either (a) appraisal genuinely dominates registration — supports this project's H1 — or (b) their specific instruments were noisy/underpowered for those constructs — in which case this project's own instruments (MAIA-2/BPQ vs. ASI-3) might also underperform for reasons unrelated to the theoretical claim. Worth naming this ambiguity if asked, rather than treating the 2022 null as confirming evidence.

**Lesson for next time a literature summary arrives from an external source (another AI, a search tool, a person)**: real-sounding author names attached to real-sounding journals is not sufficient — this batch had one paper that was real but mis-cited (wrong publication venue/status), two that were real but with unconfirmable specific findings, and one (Neacsiu) that we wrongly called fabricated on 2026-09-03 and only found four days later using a different lookup tool (OpenAlex succeeded where PubMed/PMC/Google search had failed, likely because the paper was very recently indexed). **Amended lesson**: "could not find it after several searches" is evidence of a *search* failure, not proof of *fabrication* — the correct verdict for a citation that resists verification is "unconfirmed, keep looking with more tools" rather than "fabricated," and a paper should not be permanently written off without trying at least one structured metadata API (OpenAlex, Europe PMC, Semantic Scholar) in addition to search engines and publisher pages.
