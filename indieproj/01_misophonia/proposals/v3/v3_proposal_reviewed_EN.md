## Executive Summary

**One sentence**: this is a well-instrumented, honestly-scoped psychometric decomposition study with real statistical teeth at the trial level (Layer 2), but it is currently sold with causal/mechanistic language ("gating," a causal-chain diagram) that its cross-sectional, self-report-heavy design cannot support, and it has not yet applied to its own central distinction (registration vs. appraisal) the same discriminant-validity scrutiny the author has already learned to demand of other people's work.

**What's strong and should not change**: the three-layer escalation structure (questionnaire → trial-level → neural), the decision to carry the interaction/"gating" claim on the trial-level model (H5, ≈3,360 observations) rather than the underpowered participant-level one (H3), the pre-registered subscale-selection rules, and the willingness to state the novelty downgrade explicitly (competitive test of an existing model, not a new framework).

**What's weak, in order of how much it would cost the paper if unaddressed**:
1. Registration (S) and appraisal (A), as operationalized (MAIA-2/BPQ vs. ASI-3), have not been shown to be *discriminable* from each other or from general negative affectivity — the same disattenuated-correlation / two-factor-CFA check the author already ran on a labmate's stress-vs-internalizing distinction has not been run on this proposal's own two core constructs.
2. The causal-chain figure ("sensation → registered → appraised → severity") licenses a temporal/mechanistic reading the design does not test; every confirmatory analysis in the document is a between-person or cross-level correlational moderation, not a test of sequence.
3. "Gating" is used as if it names a mechanism; it currently names a statistical interaction term. These are not the same claim, and a computational-psychiatry reviewer will say so directly.
4. The strongest framing candidate the author has already identified — explaining a previously-published, data-driven anticipatory/reactive subgroup structure — lives in a separate, unread-source file and has not been merged into this proposal, which is still running on the weaker "competitive quantitative test" framing.
5. Layer 3's anatomical hypothesis (anterior vs. posterior/mid insula) is stated without a pre-specified ROI definition procedure, which is exactly the degree of freedom that turns a pre-registered neuroimaging hypothesis into a data-dependent one.

**Verdict**: fundable and executable as Layers 1–2 stand; needs one afternoon of tightening (discriminant-validity check, causal-language audit, ROI pre-specification) before it should go in front of anyone who reviews interoception papers for a living.

## Revised Full Research Plan

### 1. Background

Misophonia — a strong aversive and often physiological reaction to specific sounds (chewing, breathing, tapping) — sits at an unresolved classification boundary: proposed variously as an auditory-processing disorder, an anxiety-spectrum condition, and an OCD-spectrum condition, with no DSM entry to adjudicate the question. A 2025 cognitive model proposed that reaction intensity is shaped by the *misinterpretation* of bodily sensations accompanying the trigger sound, and a 2026 study found anxiety sensitivity (ASI-3) predicts misophonia severity in an OCD sample. Neither study measured interoception directly or entered a registration-level term alongside the appraisal-level one, leaving open whether the appraisal effect is independent of how strongly the underlying sensation is registered in the first place — the question this plan addresses.

This sits inside a broader, active research program on how bodily-signal processing relates to psychiatric symptomatology — spanning basic interoceptive neuroscience, insula function across diagnostic categories, and causal manipulation of visceral rhythms to test their behavioral consequences. The present study does not claim methodological parity with that program's strongest designs (which include causal pharmacological manipulation and diagnosis-spanning meta-analysis); it claims a narrower, defensible thing — that the registration/appraisal decomposition, tested with existing psychometric and trial-level behavioral data in a single well-characterized clinical sample, is a genuine and previously untested increment.

**Recent misophonia-specific neuroimaging (2026, verified 2026-09-03 — see `01_misophonia/literature/NOVELTY_AUDIT_ADDENDUM_20260903.md` for the full verification log)**: Ajmera, Khan et al. (2026, *Hearing Research*) report altered intrinsic functional connectivity in misophonia with and without hyperacusis comorbidity, and Jain, Ajmera et al. (2026, *Cognitive, Affective, & Behavioral Neuroscience*) report a task-based fMRI comparison of affective sound processing in the same two groups — both papers are confirmed real and on-topic, and both support the general premise that misophonia has a distinguishable neural signature beyond auditory processing per se. **Two caveats before either is cited for a specific claim**: (1) their exact reported findings (which networks, which direction of effect) could not be independently confirmed in this review — PMC/PubMed access was blocked at verification time — so any specific claim from these papers should be re-checked against the actual full text before it appears in a written proposal or talk; (2) shared authorship (Ajmera appears on both) means these are two related papers from one lab, not two independent lines of evidence, and should not be cited as if they were. A third citation — an fMRI insula–dmPFC connectivity study attributed to Neacsiu et al., *BMC Psychiatry*, 2026 — was initially unconfirmable (2026-09-03) and is now verified (2026-09-07) via OpenAlex: Neacsiu, Gerlus, Graner, Bukhari-Parlakturk, Choi, Rosenthal & LaBar, "Patterns of shared and differential neural activity in misophonia and non-misophonic clinical emotional dysregulation," *BMC Psychiatry* (DOI 10.1186/s12888-026-08331-3, PMID 42374317). The paper compares 29 adults with misophonia to 30 adults with high (non-misophonic) emotional dysregulation and reports that right insula–dorsomedial prefrontal cortex (dmPFC) connectivity distinguishes the misophonia group, with this connectivity pattern differing between passive listening to trigger sounds and active cognitive reappraisal. This directly supports a disorder-specific, insula-centered neural signature and a specificity test against non-misophonic emotional dysregulation, consistent with this proposal's Layer 3 framing. Caveat: the finding above is reconstructed from an abstract via a metadata API, not read from the full text, so exact statistics/effect sizes remain unconfirmed pending a full-text read — cite the qualitative pattern, not specific numbers, until then.

### 2. Core Scientific Question

> **Within misophonia, does the appraisal of a bodily reaction as threatening predict clinical severity over and above how strongly that reaction is registered — and if so, does that appraisal effect operate as a gate (registration converts to symptom severity only when appraised as threatening) rather than as an independent additive contribution?**

### 3. Hypotheses

Organized by evidentiary layer, each layer independently deliverable, each with its confirmatory/exploratory status stated up front — unchanged from v2's discipline, with two additions (3.4, a formal discriminant-validity test of S and A themselves; and 3.5, an explicit alternative-model comparison).

**3.1 Layer 1 — Participant level (questionnaire; confirmatory for H1/H2/H4, exploratory for H3)**

- H1 (main effect): ASI-3 predicts AMISOS-R controlling for MAIA-2/BPQ-SF-A.
- H1b (relative importance): variance in AMISOS-R attributable to S vs. A, via a dominance-analysis or Shapley-value decomposition that does not depend on predictor ordering — LMG or Shapley regression, not sequential ΔR², given MAIA/ASI-3 are correlated.
- H2 (discriminant validity vs. adjacent constructs): H1 survives controlling for SPSQ (general sensory sensitivity) and OCI-R (OCD-spectrum symptoms).
- H3 (participant-level interaction, exploratory, explicitly underpowered): MAIA × ASI-3 → AMISOS-R, reported as an effect estimate with confidence intervals, no significance claim.
- H4 (outcome specificity): the S/A structure predicts AMISOS-R better than it predicts IDAS-II total distress.

**3.2 Layer 2 — Trial level (in-scanner behavioral log; confirmatory, principal carrier of the gating claim)**

- H5: `P(rated "unpleasant") ~ SoundType × ASI-3 + (1 + SoundType | Participant)`, ordinal mixed model over ≈3,360 trial-level observations (42 sounds × 80 participants). Core test: does ASI-3 specifically amplify aversion to trigger sounds without amplifying aversion to the built-in unpleasant-sound control?
- H6: the same model with MAIA × SoundType added; predicted that ASI-3's moderation exceeds MAIA's.

**3.3 Layer 3 — Neural (pre-registered, contingent on preprocessing availability and PI coordination; not part of the one-year deliverable unless that coordination resolves)**

- H7 (anatomical dissociation): AMISOS-R associates more strongly with anterior-insula response during the interoceptive attention task than with posterior/mid-insula response; ASI-3 associates primarily with anterior insula, MAIA primarily with posterior/mid insula.
- **H7b (new — folds in the previously parked heart/gut modality question)**: within the same interoceptive attention task, the heart and gut conditions are not interchangeable "interoception" — if the registration/appraisal account is right, the heart-vs-gut contrast should interact with the anterior/posterior gradient rather than only with condition-type main effects. Because a disgust-specific outcome does not exist anywhere in this dataset (verified against the full 14-questionnaire battery and the three-category trial rating — see `01_misophonia/现有数据一览.md`), H7b substitutes AMISOS-R severity and the IDAS-II Panic+Social Anxiety composite as outcomes in place of a disgust-specific measure. This does not test the disgust-specific dissociation from the domperidone/gastric-rhythm literature; it tests a weaker, available claim (does the heart/gut contrast carry independent registration/appraisal information beyond the anterior/posterior gradient alone) and should be described as such, not as a replication of that literature.

**3.4 New — discriminant validity of S and A as constructs (add before any other analysis is run)**

Before H1 is tested, establish that MAIA-2/BPQ-SF-A (S) and ASI-3 (A) are empirically separable, using the same method already applied in this project's review of a labmate's stress/internalizing distinction:
- Disattenuated correlation: `r_disattenuated = r_observed(S,A) / sqrt(reliability_S × reliability_A)`, using each instrument's own reported or sample-computed Cronbach's α/ω. A disattenuated r approaching 1 would mean S and A are not separable constructs in this sample regardless of what the labels imply.
- Two-factor vs. one-factor CFA comparison: does a model with S and A as separate correlated factors fit significantly better (χ² difference test, plus CFI/RMSEA) than a model collapsing all S and A items onto one factor?
- If either check fails, H1/H1b/H3 must be reframed as "one construct predicting AMISOS-R with two overlapping measurement instruments," not as a dissociation — this is a possible finding, not a proposal-killing one, but it changes what can be claimed.

**3.5 New — explicit alternative model**

State and pre-register at least one competing account the S×A model must outperform, not merely control for: a single general-negative-affectivity model in which a common higher-order trait (shared variance across ASI-3, MAIA subscales tapping worry, IDAS-II Dysphoria) predicts AMISOS-R, with S and A entering only as this shared factor's indicators. Compare via AIC/BIC or non-nested model tests, not via sequential covariate entry. This directly addresses the discriminant-validity finding from 3.4 rather than assuming it away.

### 4. Methods / Analytic Framework

**4.1 Data (confirmed inventory — see `01_misophonia/现有数据一览.md` for the full table; the items below were unresolved in v2 and are now settled)**

- N = 80 (50 clinical / 30 control), drawn without new recruitment from a prior N=159 behavioral study (145 consented to recontact).
- All 14 online questionnaires are now identified by name and item count (ASI-3·18, MAIA-2·37, BPQ-SF-A·26, AMISOS-R·12, SPSQ·16, OCI-R·18, IDAS-II·99, K-CTQ·28, LTE-Q·12, K-BDEFS-SF·20, K-SADS-PL-Tic·48, demographics·7, PANAS·24, ACS·20). No alexithymia scale exists in the battery; the three previously-unconfirmed instruments (demographics, PANAS, ACS) contain no emotion-type or disgust-specific content, closing a question v2 left open.
- The auditory task (42 sounds; trigger/unpleasant/neutral, ~15 s each, three-category rating) is confirmed as an fMRI task, but the behavioral rating output is separable from the imaging pipeline and requires no preprocessing — Layer 2's premise stands.
- The interoceptive attention task (heart/gut/visual-control, ~10 s per block) is confirmed to be **fMRI-only with no independent accuracy or confidence output** — this was previously stated as a design correction in v2 (§3.3) and is now confirmed at the protocol level, not inferred. Layer 3, including H7b, is unreachable without imaging preprocessing; there is no version of it that stays inside the "no fMRI preprocessing" constraint.

**4.2 Discriminant-validity and alternative-model checks** — see 3.4–3.5.

**4.3 Statistical conventions** (retained from v2, unchanged): HC3 robust SEs; Bonferroni across the primary family (H1, H1b, H2, H4), FDR with q-values for H3/exploratory; age/sex/group as covariates; effect sizes and CIs reported alongside any p-value; subscale selection fixed in advance (MAIA-2 Noticing + Body Listening primary, others exploratory; ASI-3 Physical Concerns primary; IDAS-II total primary).

**4.4 Cross-level convergence test (new)** — v2 asserts H6's trial-level moderation will "converge" with H1's participant-level main effect but specifies no formal test of that convergence. Add a multilevel decomposition (within-person-centered ASI-3 vs. between-person ASI-3 as separate predictors, following the same within/between separation logic used elsewhere in this project's methodological toolkit) so that "convergence" is a stated, testable comparison of within- and between-person coefficients rather than an assumption.

**4.5 Layer 3 ROI pre-specification (new, updated 2026-09-03)** — before any imaging data are inspected, register how "anterior insula" and "posterior/mid insula" will be operationalized. Peaks selected from the interoceptive-attention contrast itself are not an option — that would be circular with the hypothesis test run on the same contrast. Two independent, literature-based sources are proposed, cross-checked against each other rather than relied on singly:

- **Posterior/mid insula ROI**: coordinates from Nord, Lawson & Dalgleish (2021, *American Journal of Psychiatry*), "Disrupted Dorsal Mid-Insula Activation During Interoception Across Psychiatric Disorders" — this meta-analysis is already verified and cited in `../NORD_ALIGNMENT.md`, and its dorsal mid-insula locus is the most directly relevant published coordinate for the registration-level ROI in this design. **The exact MNI coordinates have not yet been extracted from the paper and must be read from the original before the mask is built** — this proposal does not assert specific numbers it has not verified.
- **Anterior insula ROI**: no single equally on-target meta-analytic coordinate has been verified yet for the appraisal-level locus. Two options to evaluate before locking the mask: (a) a published interoceptive-accuracy or anterior-insula-specific meta-analysis (to be identified and read in full, not assumed), or (b) a term-based meta-analytic association map from Neurosynth or NeuroQuery (e.g., the "interoception" or "insula" term maps, built from thousands of independently published fMRI studies' reported coordinates) — this is the appropriate use of a large-aggregate, literature-based tool for defining an anatomically grounded functional ROI, unlike a stimulus-encoding model such as TRIBE, which was evaluated for this purpose and rejected: TRIBE's generalization is scoped to naturalistic audiovisual/text stimuli it was trained on and to stimulus-conditioned group-average prediction, neither of which matches this task's short instructed interoceptive-attention blocks or this design's individual-differences question (does trait-level appraisal/registration predict the strength of a person's own regional response). Neurosynth/NeuroQuery, by contrast, aggregate coordinates actually reported from studies using comparable interoception paradigms — the right kind of literature-based generalization for this specific step.
- **Cross-check**: before finalizing, confirm the two sources' loci are anatomically distinct (not overlapping within the same insular subregion) and both fall within standard insula parcellation boundaries (e.g., the Brainnetome or Deen et al. 2011 insula subdivision atlas), so the eventual dissociation test is not comparing two masks that already overlap.

**4.6 External construct-validity citation: MATA (new, 2026-09-03)** — the Misophonia Audiovisual Trigger Archive (Oh, Palmer, Sabatina, Pietrini, O'Reilly & Shinkareva; **bioRxiv preprint, posted 2025-09-15, DOI 10.1101/2025.09.09.675007 — not yet peer-reviewed or published in a journal; do not cite it as a *Scientific Data* publication, that status is unconfirmed**) is a public archive of 1,300 five-second audiovisual clips across 12 empirically validated trigger categories, standardized (mono, noise-reduced, loudness-normalized to −23 LUFS), with independent validation data from 26 misophonia and 26 control participants showing trigger stimuli rated as significantly more distressing than other aversive stimuli (*p* < .001). **Use in this proposal**: cited here only as external, independent replication evidence that trigger-specific distress is a measurable, reproducible phenomenon across different stimulus sets and labs — this supports the construct validity of treating trigger/unpleasant/neutral as a meaningful three-way distinction in the existing CAD Lab auditory task (Layer 2), without requiring any new data collection. **Not used as a new stimulus or data source**: building an independent online replication study using MATA stimuli (e.g., via Prolific/CloudResearch) is a real, separate option someone could pursue, but it would constitute new data collection and falls outside this proposal's zero-new-collection constraint (§2) — noted here as a possible future/independent project, not folded into the one-year plan.

### 5. Expected Results (falsifiability statement for each layer)

| Result pattern | What it means |
|---|---|
| H1/H2 hold; H3.4 discriminant check passes | Registration and appraisal are separable, and appraisal carries independent predictive weight — the core claim is supported at the trait level |
| H1 holds but 3.4 fails (S, A not discriminable) | The proposal's title claim needs revision to "a general bodily-threat-sensitivity factor predicts severity"; still publishable, different framing |
| H1 fails, H5 holds | Trait-level appraisal doesn't predict severity, but trial-level appraisal-sensitivity does — suggests the effect is state/context-dependent, not a stable trait, itself a finding worth reporting |
| H5 fails | The gating claim is not supported at adequate power; H3's underpowered estimate becomes the only (weak) evidence, and the paper should say so plainly rather than lean on H3 |
| H7/H7b (contingent) hold | Anatomical dissociation converges with the behavioral one — the strongest possible version of the paper, but explicitly a stretch goal outside the one-year plan |

### 6. Significance

**6.1 Novelty positioning.** Two framings currently coexist across this project's files and should be merged rather than left as competitors: (a) v2's "first competitive quantitative test of an existing model's central claim, entering registration and appraisal simultaneously" and (b) the README's currently-stronger, currently-unintegrated candidate — "an unsupervised, data-driven anticipatory/reactive subgroup structure has already been found in misophonia by prior work using no theoretical model; this study tests whether the registration/appraisal decomposition explains that already-validated structure." Framing (b) requires no novelty argument (the structure is already shown to exist), no argument that the distinction matters (the clustering already demonstrates it matters empirically), and is falsifiable in a cleaner way (group membership prediction rather than a continuous interaction). **Before this plan is finalized, read the source paper defining that subgroup structure** (flagged as unread in `README.md`) and decide whether framing (b) replaces framing (a) as the paper's stated contribution, or whether both are reported as complementary confirmatory targets.

**6.2 Feasibility within the one-year, zero-new-collection, no-fMRI-preprocessing constraint.** Layers 1 and 2 fit entirely inside the constraint as stated and do not depend on any items still to be confirmed. Layer 3, including H7b, does not fit inside the constraint as currently defined and requires two separate resolutions before it can be attempted: PI coordination on the shared interoceptive-imaging aim (open meeting question), and a deliberate decision to accept the fMRI-preprocessing time cost this year rather than next. Recommend keeping Layer 3 explicitly out of the one-year deliverable, pre-registered but not scheduled, exactly as v2 already does — this reviewed plan does not change that recommendation, only sharpens what would need to be true for it to change.

**6.3 Clinical implication, stated conservatively.** If appraisal (not registration) is the dominant driver, the clinical implication is that intervention should target the evaluation of the bodily reaction (cognitive reappraisal, interoceptive exposure with reappraisal framing) rather than sensory desensitization alone — but this implication should not appear in the abstract or introduction stated as more than a hypothesis-contingent inference, since a cross-sectional dissociation design cannot itself establish that changing appraisal would change severity; that requires an intervention study this plan does not include.

## Critical Comments (reviewer pass)

**Major 1 — Discriminant validity of the central constructs is asserted, not tested.** The whole paper rests on S and A being separable. Section 6 (Known threats) in v2 tests whether the *effect* survives controlling for SPSQ and OCI-R, but never asks whether MAIA-2/BPQ-SF-A and ASI-3 are separable from *each other*. Given that anxiety-sensitivity and interoceptive-sensibility measures are known in the broader literature to correlate substantially, this is the single most likely point of failure, and it is checkable with data already in hand before any confirmatory test is run. This should be analysis step zero, not an afterthought.

**Major 2 — The causal-chain diagram overclaims relative to the design.** "Trigger sound → registered → appraised → severity" reads as a temporal cascade. Every hypothesis that actually gets tested (H1–H6) is a correlational or cross-level moderation claim among variables measured at the same session or within the same ~15 s trial window, with no measurement of registration and appraisal as temporally ordered events. This is the same distinction the author has already applied carefully to someone else's cross-lagged panel design (within-person association is not inherently directional; same-wave association is symmetric, not sequential) — it should be applied here with equal rigor. Recommend either (a) softening the figure to a conceptual/theoretical diagram explicitly labeled as such, separate from the empirical model, or (b) if a genuinely sequential test is wanted, specifying what in the existing data could support ordering (e.g., does the trial structure record a separable "notice" response before the pleasantness rating? If not, say plainly that ordering is not tested this year).

**Major 3 — "Gating" needs to be a stated hypothesis about form, not a name for an interaction term.** A significant multiplicative interaction is consistent with gating but equally consistent with a simple additive amplification that happens to be nonlinear in the chosen scale, or with a threshold effect at only one tail of the ASI-3 distribution. If "gating" is meant literally (registration only converts to symptoms above some appraisal threshold), that predicts a specific functional form — simple-slope analysis at high vs. low ASI-3 (already a familiar tool in this project's toolkit from the discriminant-validity work) should be pre-registered as the way "gating" will be distinguished from "amplification," rather than inferred post hoc from whichever interaction shape the data happen to show.

**Major 4 — Internal inconsistency: the strongest available framing is not in this document.** The unsupervised-subgroup framing in `README.md` is described there as "currently the strongest candidate" and requires reading one source paper that has not yet been read. Submitting or presenting v3 without resolving this is presenting a self-assessed second-best framing as the plan of record. This is a one-afternoon fix (read the paper, decide whether it replaces or supplements the current framing) that meaningfully changes the paper's novelty argument — it should happen before, not after, the plan is locked.

**Minor 1 — Layer 3 ROI definition is not yet falsification-proof.** "Anterior vs. posterior/mid insula" needs a named, pre-specified operationalization (atlas + coordinates, or an independent localizer) before any imaging data are seen, or the eventual anatomical dissociation claim will be vulnerable to the same post hoc-peak-selection critique the field already treats with suspicion.

**Minor 2 — Check ASI-3 Physical Concerns and AMISOS-R item content for construct overlap.** Not verified in this review — flagged as a check to run, not a confirmed problem — but misophonia-severity scales sometimes include items about the intensity of one's own physiological/catastrophic reaction to the trigger, which would be conceptually adjacent to ASI-3's items about fearing physical anxiety symptoms. If overlap exists, H1 risks partial item-level redundancy rather than a substantive relationship between separable constructs. A five-minute read of both instruments' item lists resolves this.

**Minor 3 — Power estimates may be optimistic under range restriction.** Section 6 of v2 already and correctly flags that psychiatric exclusion criteria create a supernormal control group and truncate the distress distribution. The power table in §5.3, however, is not adjusted for that truncation — the 74–80% power figures assume population effect sizes that the truncation itself would attenuate. Either present power estimates as an upper bound explicitly, or run a sensitivity analysis at a range of plausible attenuated effect sizes.

**Minor 4 — H7b should not be described using "dissociation" language borrowed from the disgust-specific literature it deliberately does not replicate.** Since the outcome variables were substituted precisely because a disgust-specific measure does not exist, H7b tests a different, weaker claim than the causal gastric-rhythm/disgust work it is adjacent to. Calling it a "dissociation" invites readers to assume the disgust-specific result generalizes; it should be named for what it tests (does the heart/gut contrast add information beyond the anterior/posterior gradient for anxiety- and severity-related outcomes).

**What would make this an easy accept rather than a hard one**: run 3.4 first. If S and A are discriminable, this becomes a clean, honestly-scoped, well-powered-where-it-matters paper. If they are not, the paper still exists, just under a different and more modest claim — and finding that out now, with existing data, costs nothing compared to finding it out after Layer 3 is attempted.
