# Literature Review — Registration/Appraisal Model in Misophonia

> Prepared for review with Prof. Juyoen Hur. All citations below were checked directly against original sources (PubMed/PMC/journal pages) before inclusion.

## What is already established

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Bodily/interoceptive sensitivity (ASI-3 Physical Concerns) is elevated in a misophonia-defined profile, in a large community sample | Community-sample profile analysis, N=628, *Behaviour Research and Therapy*, 2018 ([PubMed 29530266](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29530266/)) |
| Anxiety sensitivity relates to misophonia and to aggression dimensions | *Evaluating anxiety sensitivity as a moderator of misophonia and dimensions of aggression*, 2021 ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2211364921000373)) |
| Anxiety sensitivity and distress intolerance relate to misophonia maintenance/treatment in a youth sample | Cepeda et al., *Journal of Clinical Psychology*, 2026 ([Wiley 10.1002/jclp.70095](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jclp.70095)) |

## The closest existing precedent

Wang, Vitoratou et al. (2022), *Emotion Processes Predicting Outbursts and Functional Impact in Misophonia*, N=703 (315 misophonia), *Frontiers in Psychology* ([PMC9292922](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9292922/); [KCL portal](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/emotion-processes-predicting-outbursts-and-functional-impact-in-m/)).

This study modeled anxiety sensitivity, interoception, emotional threat appraisal, and externalising appraisal together against two outcomes: misophonic outbursts and functional impact. Externalising appraisal predicted outbursts; emotional threat appraisal predicted functional impact. **Anxiety sensitivity and interoception were not significant independent predictors of either outcome once the appraisal variables were in the model.**

This is the single most relevant piece of prior evidence for the present design: it is consistent with appraisal carrying more predictive weight than raw bodily sensitivity, though it used different outcomes (outbursts/impairment, not clinical severity) and different instruments, and does not itself establish the registration/appraisal structure proposed here.

## The gap

No published study has:
1. entered a registration-type measure (bodily-sensibility, e.g. MAIA-2/BPQ) and an appraisal-type measure (e.g. ASI-3) as a single competing model against misophonia **severity** specifically;
2. established, prior to that test, that the two measures are **empirically discriminable constructs** rather than overlapping proxies for the same underlying trait;
3. tested an explicit **gating form** (registration converts to severity only when appraised as threatening) as a pre-specified functional pattern, distinct from simple additive amplification.

## Novelty statement

> Prior work has independently implicated interoceptive/bodily sensitivity, anxiety sensitivity, and cognitive-emotional appraisal in misophonia, and the closest precedent (Wang, Vitoratou et al., 2022) found that anxiety sensitivity and interoception drop out as predictors once appraisal-type variables are modeled — but no study has entered registration and appraisal as a single competing model against misophonia severity, established that the two are discriminable constructs, and tested whether appraisal gates (rather than merely adds to) the conversion of registered bodily sensation into clinical severity.

## Recent neuroimaging context (background only)

Two 2026 papers report altered neural signatures in misophonia beyond simple auditory processing — Ajmera, Khan et al. (*Hearing Research*) on resting-state connectivity, and Jain, Ajmera et al. (*Cognitive, Affective, & Behavioral Neuroscience*) on task-based responses to affective sounds. Both are cited here as general background supporting a distinguishable neural profile in misophonia; their specific findings have not yet been independently re-verified against full text, so no specific claim from either paper is relied on in the hypotheses below.

## Related resource (external validation, not a data source)

**MATA (Misophonia Audiovisual Trigger Archive)** — Oh, Palmer, Sabatina, Pietrini, O'Reilly, Shinkareva, *bioRxiv preprint* (posted 2025-09-15, DOI 10.1101/2025.09.09.675007). 1,300 standardized five-second audiovisual trigger clips across 12 categories, validated on 26 misophonia + 26 control participants. Cited in the full proposal (§4.6) only as independent construct-validity evidence that trigger-specific distress replicates across stimulus sets and labs — not used as a stimulus set or data source here, so it does not affect the zero-new-collection constraint. Flagged as a preprint, not a peer-reviewed publication.
