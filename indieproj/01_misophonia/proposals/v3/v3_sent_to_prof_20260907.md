The question: is misophonia severity driven more by how strongly bodily sensations are registered, or by how threateningly they're appraised — and does appraisal gate that conversion (registration only becomes severity when appraised as threatening), rather than just adding to it independently?

The design, in one line: using only CAD Lab misophonia data (N=80, no new collection), registration is operationalized as MAIA-2/BPQ-SF-A, appraisal as ASI-3, and severity as AMISOS-R, tested across three layers —

Layer
Data
What it tests
1 · Questionnaire
MAIA-2/BPQ vs. ASI-3 → AMISOS-R
Does appraisal predict severity independent of registration?
2 · Trial-level
42-sound auditory task, ≈3,360 observations
Does appraisal specifically amplify aversion to trigger sounds (not just any unpleasant sound)? — this is where the gating claim is actually powered to be tested
3 · Neural
Interoceptive attention task, anterior vs. posterior/mid insula
Does the same registration/appraisal split show up anatomically?
Why this is a real gap: bodily sensitivity and anxiety sensitivity have each been linked to misophonia separately, and the closest precedent (Wang, Vitoratou et al., 2022, N=703) found that once appraisal-type variables are modeled alongside them, anxiety sensitivity and interoception stop being significant independent predictors — suggestive of exactly this pattern, but tested against different outcomes (outbursts/impairment, not severity) with different instruments. No study has entered registration and appraisal as one competing model against severity specifically, confirmed they're separable constructs, and tested the gating form. Recent (2026) misophonia neuroimaging is broadly consistent with a distinguishable neural signature beyond auditory processing, though I'm treating those papers as background only until I've verified their specific findings against full text.
